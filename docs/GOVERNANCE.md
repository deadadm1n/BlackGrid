# BlackGrid — Governance and community authorization

**Status:** approved design direction; exact thresholds remain open. Do not invent them.

### 4.4 Community governance
Players can propose **any Minecraft-related change**; eligibility review and policy decide whether it can be balloted and automatically executed. Examples: server creation/type, server retirement/shutdown (not deletion), world reset with preserved snapshot, difficulty, datapacks, plugin request, event, rules, economics. Votes have scope (network / instance), immutable revision per ballot round with authorized edits triggering a new ballot round, electorate snapshot, unique eligible voter identity, timestamps, quorum, threshold, deterministic counting, and immutable outcome. Identity linking and anti-alt/anti-sybil policy are essential; thresholds/quorums/eligibility are **owner-configurable and unset until policy is chosen**. Owner defines which actions are automatically executable, manual-review, or prohibited. Passed-but-unsupported proposal becomes a tracked feature request, not an improvised privileged AI action.

**Ballot timing and notification:** Vote duration is configurable per ballot only within an inclusive minimum of 24 hours and maximum of 72 hours (three days), measured by UTC timestamps; local Discord displays may show viewer-local time. At ballot activation, BlackGrid must durably create and publish a notification to the designated Discord announcement channel for the network or affected server, including the proposal summary, voting link/buttons, eligibility/scope, opening time, and closing time. The notification is mandatory: use a persisted outbox, idempotent delivery, retry/backoff and operator alerts. A ballot must not become open for voting until initial publication is confirmed; if publication fails, keep it `PENDING_ANNOUNCEMENT` and do not start its voting clock. Record the Discord message ID to avoid duplicates. On cancellation, also post a cancellation notification; on closure, publish results. If Discord is offline, drafts and proposals remain usable but new ballots await announcement. No quiet/hidden ballots.

**Discord administrator veto:** Any member holding Discord's actual guild Administrator permission at the time of the action may use a protected `/vote cancel` command or authenticated dashboard control to cancel a vote. Role names alone do not confer this authority. Core must verify the administrator's current guild identity and permission via trusted Discord state, not message text or AI claims. Cancellable votes transition atomically to `CANCELLED_BY_ADMIN`, with optional reason, administrator ID, time, proposal/ballot ID, and an announcement. Closing or tally workers must recheck cancellation inside the same transactional authorization boundary before scheduling execution. Cancelled ballots never authorize actions; cast ballots remain in history. Cancellation is not reversal of an action that has already executed; execution/rollback is a separate owner-controlled workflow. If the vote passed but the resulting action remains queued, the administrator may cancel the *pending vote-authorized operation* before execution begins. If execution has begun, stop only at a declared safe checkpoint and escalate instead of claiming an impossible instant rollback. A vote is never silently erased.

### 4.4a Rules acknowledgement, proposal revisions, and unified permissions (approved design; not implementation authorization)

**Mandatory rule acceptance:** Members must be shown the current server/community rules and explicitly accept their version before creating or casting a vote. Persist Discord user ID, rules version/hash and acceptance timestamp; validate at the backend on every action, not just through channel visibility. Rules-version changes can require re-acceptance under owner-configured policy. Membership/linked-player eligibility and scope constraints still apply independently. No silently inferred acceptance.

**Proposal form:** Eligible creators submit four required fields: **Vote Title**, **Vote Request** (the actual approval sought), **Request Details** (supporting explanation), and **Time Limit** (24, 48, or 72 hours, within the established inclusive 24–72-hour policy). Record creator, intended network/instance scope, and immutable version identifier. Apply separate controls for permission to create vs permission to cast; configurable anti-spam cooldowns.

**Editable proposals and reset semantics:** Authorized users may edit an *open* proposal's fields according to field-specific permissions. Every submitted content edit, including a clarification or administrator-added constraint, produces a new immutable proposal version; atomically supersedes the current ballot round; invalidates all ballots from that round (retained in historical audit, never reused for tally); and requires a **new full-duration voting round** with fresh Discord announcement and re-voting. The selected time limit on the revised version must still be 24–72 hours. If announcement is delayed, the new timer does not begin until delivery confirmation. The Discord update must identify editor, modified fields and meaningful before/after differences, explain prior votes are void and provide a new voting link. No silent edits to live ballot content. Once the ballot has finished or been cancelled, revisions require a new proposal. Repeated edits may be bounded by administrator-managed rate limits to prevent indefinite cycling; administrators retain their immediate cancellation powers. Historical ballot rounds remain immutable.

**Administrative constraints vs author text:** Maintain separately attributed proposal sections: original author request/details, administrator clarifications, and enforceable implementation constraints. Example: an administrator may add “chickens may share flock memory but must not share global memory” to THE HUNT; this cannot masquerade as original author wording. Such a constraint changes the version and restarts voting with cleared votes. Conflicting instructions or policy restrictions are flagged for review.

**Unified granular authorization:** BlackGrid Core is the authoritative policy engine for Discord, website, instance operations, proposals, and AI. Discord roles map to BlackGrid principals/roles but do not substitute for backend authorization. Support network-wide and instance-scoped grants with deny-by-default and consistent enforcement across API endpoints, workers, Discord commands, and dashboard—not merely hidden controls. At minimum define `vote.create`, `vote.cast`, `vote.edit.own`, `vote.edit.title`, `vote.edit.request`, `vote.edit.details`, `vote.edit.constraints`, `vote.cancel`, `vote.manage`, and `permissions.manage`; scope and ownership checks are evaluated together. Define distinct privileges for creator, moderator, administrator, community Deadhead authority, owner break-glass, and AI/service principals, without granting AI self-modification of permissions. Preserve the invariant that **any currently verified Discord guild Administrator can cancel an active ballot**, regardless of ordinary role mappings; never let a delegated custom role override that safety path. Permission grants/revocations, edits, cancellations and attempts denied by policy are audited.

**Acceptance:** A member who has not acknowledged rules cannot submit or cast a vote; an acknowledged eligible member can submit the four-field form; an authorized creator/admin edit clears previous votes, resets the full clock only after announced publication, and displays attributed revision history; a moderator without `vote.edit.constraints` cannot add constraints; a server-specific permission does not grant network-wide rights; current Discord Administrators can still veto; OpenCode offline does not affect any of these deterministic operations.

### 4.4b AI-originated combined proposals
BlackGrid's AI co-administrator may independently identify complementary player requests, recurring patterns, or connected ideas and draft a **new combined proposal**, including genuinely new game modes. It does **not** need to obtain separate author consent merely to propose a combination: it may submit the idea directly into the ordinary vote workflow when it is within existing proposal permissions. The AI cannot manufacture approval or deploy by proposing.

- Preserve references, attribution, and original text/status for each contributing request; never silently alter or delete the originals. Note compatibility conflicts, tradeoffs, resource needs, and how separate features interact.
- AI-originated proposals follow the same eligibility, moderation, permissions, and security checks as any other proposal. The governance controller—not the model—publishes the required Discord notification and opens the ballot for **24–72 hours**, with normal administrator cancellation authority.
- The **scope** is determined by impact. One server's gameplay proposal uses that server's voting system and eligible electorate; shared infrastructure, a new network server or cross-server effects use the network-wide electorate. No separate governments or administrator hierarchies.
- For combined proposals with independently useful features, present the bundle and, where appropriate, optional individually votable components. Do not imply that accepting a bundle grants permission for unsupported or unsafe functionality.
- Passing a vote can authorize planned development or an already-supported action within policy, but cannot waive Sentinel, backup/world-integrity, release approval, or resource gates. Notify players of implementation results and reasons for failure.
- Limit duplicate/near-identical proposals and spam through configurable cooldowns and active-proposal deduplication. Record the AI's reasoning as an explanatory summary, not as authority.

**Acceptance:** with several related requests on the same server, the AI can issue one attributed combined proposal into that server's normal announced vote; with a proposed new server it uses the network vote; canceling the ballot blocks action; every source proposal remains accessible; passing a vote never deploys unreviewed code.

### 4.4c Discord community UX and governance lifecycle (approved design direction; details subject to PRD review)

**Community-first Discord layout:** Organize the guild into concise groups: START HERE (welcome, rules, rule acceptance, announcements, network status); COMMUNITY (general, media, events, public AI interaction); GOVERNANCE (proposal forum/board, suggestions, official active-votes announcements, results and history); PROJECTS (project forum/board and status views for development, testing and release); MINECRAFT SERVERS (automatically provisioned per-instance chat, voting and status areas); SUPPORT (ticket initiation, bug reports, player reports); and PRIVATE ADMINISTRATION (staff, AI operations, pending actions, alerts, restricted audit logs). These are **logical channel templates**, not an instruction to create them now. Prefer consolidated forums/tags/threads rather than unlimited channel proliferation as instance count grows. Channel changes must be idempotent and authorized; private channels remain private.

**Proposal as a durable object:** A proposal has a permanent ID, canonical discussion location (prefer Discord forum post), author, network/instance scope, structured request, version history, public status, linked ballots and linked project(s). Players use a pinned interactive creation control to enter Vote Title, Vote Request, Request Details and allowed Time Limit after rules acceptance. Discord modal/component limitations may require a short multi-step flow; persist partial drafts securely. The companion website reads/writes the **same authoritative proposal and voting records** and cannot create double votes.

**Discussion and edits:** Each proposal keeps a stable, accessible discussion thread for feedback, questions, and AI commentary before or during an announced voting round. Comments are **not edits** and do not reset the timer. Any authorized change to the actual proposal text or formal constraints creates a new version and superseding ballot round, clears previous votes for tally purposes, restarts the full timer after a fresh announcement, and shows a meaningful attributed diff, as specified above. Distinguish informal advice from binding administrator constraints.

**Distinct vote intents:** Differentiate **advisory opinion poll**, **development/concept authorization**, and **implementation/change authorization** in the UI and policy; clearly state what a YES authorizes. Concept approval never silently becomes production deployment authorization. A vote passing cannot bypass capacity, security, Sentinel, integrity/backup, or separate release approval policies.

**Impact briefing:** Before an actionable ballot, publish a concise scope/impact assessment where feasible: resource estimates, server and world compatibility, dependencies, downtime, expected player impact, rollback constraints, and unresolved uncertainty. BlackGrid Core supplies measured facts; optional AI analyses are labeled as estimates with evidence/confidence and cannot present invented numbers as actual measurements. A missing impact report is shown as unknown, not fabricated.

**Public project tracking:** Approved proposals that require work become linked, durable project records with clear status such as APPROVED -> PLANNING -> DEVELOPMENT -> SENTINEL TESTING -> RELEASE REVIEW -> DEPLOYMENT -> COMPLETED (plus BLOCKED/FAILED/CANCELLED); only authorized operations advance stages. Show public progress, blockers, reasons for rejection or failed rollout, and owner/AI responsibility without leaking private operational data. Project updates are synchronized to Discord status posts and the website. Approval for investigation/prototyping is not automatically approval to deploy.

**Conflict detection and governance learning:** Detect when proposed or approved changes contradict other proposals, server rules, resource budgets, or existing deployments; surface alternatives for discussion rather than silently prioritizing one community decision over another. Preserve a structured history of proposal -> work -> verified operational results; BlackGrid AI may identify correlations, draft remediation proposals and support proactive community events, but must distinguish hypotheses from demonstrated causation and honor normal voting/authorization.

**Fairness and transparency:** One valid ballot per eligible linked voter identity per round across Discord and web. Use proportionate anti-alt checks, auditable decisions and appeal avenues; avoid treating shared IPs as proof of duplicate identity. Public views provide ballot status, tallies under owner-configured visibility policy, turnout, edits, formal constraints, veto reasons and implementation outcomes. Private ballot selections and sensitive member information are access-controlled. Recognition badges/community contributions may be displayed but never automatically weight votes more heavily. Server-specific gameplay constitutions/rules may evolve through scoped votes only within network-wide security and administrative policy.

**AI as visible co-administrator:** Provide a clearly identified public AI interaction area. AI can answer questions about proposals/projects, draft events or combined proposals, point out conflicts and facilitate discussions. It has no independent power to alter binding votes, impersonate players, rewrite authored text or deploy unapproved changes. Optional AI unavailability must not block proposals, voting or project status.

**Discord interaction examples:** The pinned proposal control offers Create Proposal / My Proposals / Rules; public ballot cards display proposal scope, version, chosen duration, exact close time, current status, YES/NO controls, full details, discussion and history. Vote confirmation should be private to the voter. The official active-votes channel is announcement-only; ballot results and all superseded rounds remain accessible. After an authorized new Minecraft instance is provisioned, its Discord channels/threads are prepared automatically, without retroactively creating channels for ideas still under consideration (such as THE HUNT).

**Implementation constraint:** This section specifies desired behavior and a Discord information architecture only. Do not provision channels, start any poll, build THE HUNT, modify guild permissions or activate production based on this documentation alone.

## Tickets and member support
### 4.5 Tickets
Open/triage/needs-info/escalated/resolved/appealed/reopened. Discord private support flows and web dashboard views. Logs/evidence are access-controlled and retention-governed. AI may summarize, correlate data and propose remedies; auto-execution only for allowlisted low-risk actions. Sensitive punishment/ban appeals and disputed facts route to human review. Ticketing persists and manual workflow operates with OpenCode off.

## Community safety and staff accountability — proposed endgame requirements

**Scope-specific rules:** The same gameplay (raiding, PvP, block destruction) can be allowed on one instance and prohibited on another. Moderation evaluates current server constitution, claims and permissions, event context, and evidence. Being abrasive or disagreeing with AI is not itself a rule violation. No punishment solely from an unsupported AI inference.

**Incident lifecycle:** report/detection → evidence preservation → triage → authorized containment → human review where required → proportional action → targeted recovery if available → notification → appeal → closed/audited. An event recorder/compatible plugin must actually exist before claiming historic block-level evidence. If evidence is absent, label it absent. Restore affected areas from reliable history when practical instead of rolling back unrelated player progress.

**Staff separation:** Community helpers/moderators/admins handle people and scoped governance. Backend operators handle technical systems. A technical role does not imply moderation rights; a community role does not imply host credentials. Deadhead is the designated final community escalation authority; owner retains infrastructure emergency authority. Discord's native Administrator ballot-cancel power remains independent of custom role names.

**Accountability:** Every consequential moderation action records actor, target, scope, rule, evidence references, duration, result and appeal status. Staff must not erase records of their own actions or decide their own appeals; route appeals to a different authorized reviewer where possible. Flag anomalous staff activity for review without assuming misconduct. Define independent oversight for staff-vs-staff conflict and credential revocation/offboarding, preserving historical audit.

**Proportionate responses:** Distinguish permitted provocation from rule breaches, active griefing, harassment, abuse, spam and bot raids. Recommended progressive tools include reminders, warnings, temporary restrictions, scoped suspension and network ban; serious cases may skip steps under configured policy. Repeated or severe punishments need transparent reason and an appeal route. Player privacy and minimum evidence collection are required.

**Governance at scale:** Maintain network-wide vs instance-specific electorate boundaries; do not allow players of unrelated servers to dominate a local matter solely through network membership. Treat turnout/quorum, proposal passing thresholds, account-age criteria and voter-roll eligibility as unset owner/community decisions, not fabricated constants. Different vote intent classes (advisory, concept/development, implementation) have distinct consequences. Both Discord and web use one ballot record. Proposal discussion never triggers timer reset; formal authorized edits do.

**Community support workload:** Unified ticket queue with severity, ownership, handoff, SLA targets as configured, staff availability, private evidence, anti-abuse/rate controls and escalation when no staff member is available. No obligation for volunteer staff to remain on-call.
